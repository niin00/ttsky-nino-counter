| **Actual root cause, confirmed by direct experiment (not theory): cocotb's `RisingEdge`/`ClockCycles` resumes at the exact simulation instant of the clock edge, without a documented guarantee that the DUT's own `posedge`-triggered always block has applied its non-blocking-assignment update for that same edge yet.** Ordering between multiple processes sensitive to the same signal edge (cocotb's internal VPI callback vs. the DUT's `always @(posedge clk)`) is implementation-defined per the Verilog LRM absent an explicit `ReadOnly()` synchronization. In this iverilog+cocotb 2.0.1 combination, cocotb's callback resolves *before* the DUT's NBA settles, so every read taken immediately after `ClockCycles`/`RisingEdge` was consistently one edge stale — confirmed by an RTL-side `$display` showing the internal `count` register itself updating correctly every cycle with zero missed increments, while the Python-side read of the same value one line later was one cycle behind. **Fix, verified locally to pass all four sub-tests (reset, increment, hold, wraparound): add a 1ns settle delay (`Timer(1, unit="ns")`) *after* `ClockCycles`/`RisingEdge` and *before* reading outputs** — critically, NOT after driving inputs (that placement, tried first, does nothing — see below). | **AI got it wrong twice before getting it right; final fix is verified by direct local reproduction, not by CI passing once** | **Yes, very much, and this is the most valuable and least obvious finding in the whole exercise.** The official template's own `test.py` boilerplate (`await ClockCycles(...); assert dut.uo_out.value == ...`) uses exactly the unsafe pattern, with no `ReadOnly()` and no settle margin — this works by luck for the placeholder's purely combinational design (no clock-edge-relative NBA to race) but is silently incorrect for **any** sequential/registered design, i.e. almost every real submission.
